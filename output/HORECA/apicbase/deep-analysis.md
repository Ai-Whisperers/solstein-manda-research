## 4. Ownership & Governance

Apicbase is founder-led but VC-backed, which is the dominant governance concern for an acquirer. The cap table includes:

- **Volta Ventures** (lead investor, Series A, May 2021) — venture partner Koen De Waele holds board seat
- **Newion Partners** (seed investor, 2019, and follow-on in Series A)
- **AAA Fund** (participant in Series A)
- **BAN Flanders / Ark Angels Fund** (pre-seed/angel investors)
- **Founders** (Carl Jacobs, Julien Burlat, Kim Rothuys, Pieter Wellens — all still active per Tech.eu 2021)

Total share capital of ~€3.7M across 3 rounds implies a real liquidation preference stack (~1-2× preferred). The Series A is now 5 years old — the VC clock is ticking, which creates acquisition optionality but also means the VCs will expect a return that may exceed founder-friendly pricing. The 4-founder structure with all founders active is stable but adds complexity to any transaction.

## 7. Overall Assessment

Apicbase is a strategically important but structurally complex M&A candidate. The product fits perfectly into SolStein's Benelux platform thesis — it is the last independent F&B back-office platform after Horeko was acquired by Exact in 2022. The tech stack is excellent (AI-native, cloud-native, SOC II), and the customer base (4,000+) provides cross-sell surface for unTill and TableFever.

The primary blocker is the VC cap table. With ~$7.4M raised across 3 rounds, the liquidation preference stack (Volta, Newion, AAA) likely means founders see far less than the headline EV. The Series A is now 5 years old — the VCs want an exit, but the Series A valuation creates a floor that may exceed what SolStein can justify for a ~$2M ARR business.

The best acquisition path is a secondary purchase of VC shares (Volta, Newion) at a negotiated discount, rather than a full-entity acquisition. This would require founder cooperation but would be cheaper than buying out the full preference stack.

**Tier**: Watch (high strategic value, high structural complexity)
